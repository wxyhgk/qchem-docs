## 13.2.2 Position Intracules

(July 4, 2026)

The intracule density, $I(\mathbf{u})$, represents the probability for the
inter-electronic vector $\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2}$:

<a id="eq1005"></a>

$$
I(\mathbf{u})=\int\rho(\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{2})\;\delta(\mathbf{r}_{12}-\mathbf{u})\;d\mathbf{r}_{1}\,\mbox{d}\mathbf{r}_{2} \tag{13.3}
$$

where $\rho(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2})$ is the two-electron
density. A simpler quantity is the spherically averaged intracule density,

<a id="eq1006"></a>

$$
P(u)=\int I(\mathbf{u})\mbox{d}\Omega_{\mathbf{u}}\;, \tag{13.4}
$$

where $\Omega_{\mathbf{u}}$ is the angular part of $\mathbf{v}$, measures the
probability that two electrons are separated by a scalar distance $u=|\mathbf{u}|$. This intracule is called a position intracule.[[450]](../bib_references/bib.md#bib466)
 If
the molecular orbitals are expanded within a basis set

<a id="eq1007"></a>

$$
\psi_{a}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\mu}c_{\mu a}\phi_{\mu}(\mathbf{r}) \tag{13.5}
$$

The quantity $P(u)$ can be expressed as

<a id="eq1008"></a>

$$
P(u)=\sum\limits_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\Gamma_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}(\mu\nu\lambda\sigma)_{\mathrm{P}} \tag{13.6}
$$

where $\Gamma_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}$ is the
two-particle density matrix and $(\mu\nu\lambda\sigma)_{\mathrm{P}}$ is
the position integral

<a id="Ch13.E7"></a>

$$
(\mu\nu\lambda\sigma)_{\mathrm{P}}=\int\phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\mathbf{r})\;\phi_{\nu}(\mathbf{r})\;\phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u})\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u})\;d\mathbf{r}\;d\Omega \tag{13.7}
$$

and $\phi_{\mu}(\mathbf{r})$, $\phi_{\nu}(\mathbf{r})$, $\phi_{\lambda}(\mathbf{r})$ and
$\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})$ are basis functions. For HF wave functions, the
position intracule can be decomposed into a Coulomb component,

<a id="Ch13.E8"></a>

$$
P_{\mathrm{J}}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}D_{\mu\nu}D_{\lambda\sigma}({\mu\nu\lambda\sigma})_{\mathrm{P}} \tag{13.8}
$$

and an exchange component,

<a id="Ch13.E9"></a>

$$
P_{\mathrm{K}}(u)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\left[D_{\mu\lambda}^{\alpha}D_{\nu\sigma}^{\alpha}+D_{\mu\lambda}^{\beta}D_{\nu\sigma}^{\beta}\right]\,({\mu\nu\lambda\sigma})_{\mathrm{P}} \tag{13.9}
$$

where $D_{\mu\nu}$ *etc.* are density matrix elements. The evaluation of
$P(u)$, $P_{\mathrm{J}}(u)$ and $P_{\mathrm{K}}(u)$ within **Q-Chem** has been described in
detail in Ref. [[779]](../bib_references/bib.md#bib817)
.

Some of the moments of $P(u)$ are physically significant,[[454]](../bib_references/bib.md#bib465)
 for example

<a id="A3.S8.EGx128"></a>

$$
\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}u^{0}P(u)du \tag{13.10}
$$

where $n$ is the number of electrons and, $\mu$ is the electronic dipole moment
and $Q$ is the trace of the electronic quadrupole moment tensor. **Q-Chem** can
compute both moments and derivatives of position intracules.
