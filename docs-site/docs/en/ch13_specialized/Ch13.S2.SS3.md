## 13.2.3 Momentum Intracules

(July 4, 2026)

Analogous quantities can be defined in momentum space; $\bar{I}(\mathbf{v})$, for example,
represents the probability density for the relative momentum
$\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{p}_{1}-\mathbf{p}_{2}$:

<a id="Ch13.E14"></a>
$$
\bar{I}(\mathbf{v})=\int\pi(\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2})\;\delta(\mathbf{p}_{12}-\mathbf{v})d\mathbf{p}_{1}d\mathbf{p}_{2} \tag{13.14}
$$

where $\pi(\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2})$ momentum two-electron density. Similarly, the
spherically averaged intracule

<a id="Ch13.E15"></a>
$$
M(v)=\int\bar{I}(\mathbf{v})\mbox{d}\Omega_{\mathbf{v}} \tag{13.15}
$$

where $\Omega_{\mathbf{v}}$ is the angular part of $\mathbf{v}$, is a measure of relative
momentum $v=|\mathbf{v}|$ and is called the momentum intracule. The quantity $M(v)$
can be written as

<a id="Ch13.E16"></a>
$$
M(v)=\sum\limits_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\Gamma_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\left(\mu\nu\lambda\sigma\right)_{\mathrm{M}} \tag{13.16}
$$

where $\Gamma_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}$ is the two-particle density
matrix and $(\mu\nu\lambda\sigma)_{\mathrm{M}}$ is the momentum integral[[119]](../bib_references/bib.md#bib104)

<a id="Ch13.E17"></a>
$$
(\mu\nu\lambda\sigma)_{\mathrm{M}}=\frac{v^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\int\phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\mathbf{r})\phi_{\nu}(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{q})\phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q})\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})j_{0}(qv)\;d\mathbf{r}\;d\mathbf{q}\;d\mathbf{u} \tag{13.17}
$$

The momentum integrals only possess four-fold permutational symmetry, *i.e.*,

<a id="A3.S8.EGx129"></a>
$$
\displaystyle(\mu\nu\lambda\sigma)_{\mathrm{M}}=(\nu\mu\lambda\sigma)_{\mathrm{M}}=(\sigma\lambda\nu\mu)_{\mathrm{M}}=(\lambda\sigma\mu\nu)_{\mathrm{M}} \tag{13.18}
$$

and therefore generation of $M(v)$ is roughly twice as expensive as $P(u)$.
Momentum intracules can also be decomposed into Coulomb $M_{\mathrm{J}}(v)$ and exchange
$M_{\mathrm{K}}(v)$ components:

<a id="Ch13.E20"></a>
$$
M_{J}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}D_{\mu\nu}D_{\lambda\sigma}(\mu\nu\lambda\sigma)_{\mathrm{M}} \tag{13.20}
$$

<a id="Ch13.E21"></a>
$$
M_{\mathrm{K}}(v)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\left[D_{\mu\lambda}^{\alpha}D_{\nu\sigma}^{\alpha}+D_{\mu\lambda}^{\beta}D_{\nu\sigma}^{\beta}\right]\,(\mu\nu\lambda\sigma)_{\mathrm{M}} \tag{13.21}
$$

Again, the even-order moments are physically significant:[[119]](../bib_references/bib.md#bib104)

<a id="Ch13.E22"></a>
$$
\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}v^{0}M(v)dv=\frac{n(n-1)}{2} \tag{13.22}
$$

<a id="Ch13.E23"></a>
$$
\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}u^{0}M_{\mathrm{J}}(v)dv=\frac{n^{2}}{2} \tag{13.23}
$$

<a id="Ch13.E24"></a>
$$
\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}v^{2}P_{\mathrm{J}}(v)dv=2nE_{\mathrm{T}} \tag{13.24}
$$

<a id="Ch13.E25"></a>
$$
\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}v^{0}M_{\mathrm{K}}(v)dv=-\frac{n}{2} \tag{13.25}
$$

where $n$ is the number of electrons and $E_{\mathrm{T}}$ is the total electronic
kinetic energy. Currently, **Q-Chem** can compute $M(v)$, $M_{\mathrm{J}}(v)$ and
$M_{\mathrm{K}}(v)$ using $s$ and $p$ basis functions only. Moments are generated
using quadrature and consequently for accurate results $M(v)$ must be computed
over a large and closely spaced $v$ range.
